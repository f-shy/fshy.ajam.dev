NotesThe best shape per dollar in the band by a wide margin and the least usable of the cheap rows. 2 GB and 20 GB for USD 0.49 a month is four times the RAM of the next cheapest NAT row and 0.23 of the per-GiB price of the C-Servers row (USD 0.245 a GiB against USD 1.06), and because there is no NAT layer, every port on the /64 is reachable, so the port-mapping problem that governs the other NAT sellers does not exist here. The price is IPv6: no IPv4 address, no NATed IPv4, and the vendor offers no add-on, so a WireGuard peer on an IPv4-only access network cannot reach it. For the two stated uses that splits the row: as a health monitor that reports outward it is excellent, and as a WireGuard node it works only where IPv6 is already in place. Two things pull the confidence down. The stock count on the order page read '0 Available' for both rungs at the Poland datacenter, so the row is priced and documented but not demonstrably in stock today. And the entity needed correcting: the vendor names BREEZLE LLC of Brandon, Florida, but a Florida entity search returns no such company, while the RIPE organisation record for the AS the vendor claims gives 'reg-nr = 4921432 (Delaware)' - so the entity is a Delaware LLC mailing from a Florida address, the Florida register is the wrong place to look, and the Delaware number was not checked against the Delaware register in verification. That is a real entity with a real AS and a domain-age story, not the dissolved-company pattern this category was warned about, but it is also not a completed registry check. Not established: the registration form's fields, the payment gateway list at checkout, the renewal price after year one, and whether stock exists at any location.
