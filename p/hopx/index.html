Every sandbox can carry a timeout and is destroyed when it expires. The SDK signature gives timeout_seconds a default of 'no timeout', the docs illustrate 5 minutes to 4 hours, and the marketing page claims 'Up to 30 days runtime'. No page states a maximum, so whether a 730-hour timeout is accepted is undocumented.
Bindingvolume-quota The credit is the only published bound on duration, and at the smallest published shape it lasts 123 days of 24/7, so the first thing to fail is the balance, not the platform.
Identityemail The pricing page states 'No credit card required' and '$200 free credits'; no card, no phone and no ID are mentioned. IMPORTANT: the console at console.hopx.ai (and console.hopx.dev, which the docs link to) offers ONLY 'Connect to HopX with: Google GitHub'. A browser OAuth button pair, with no email-and-password field anywhere on the page. The account is therefore created by an OAuth identity, not by an email form. No hold exists because no payment method is taken.
EgressNo egress charge or allowance is published on the pricing page; the page prices compute, memory and storage only.
Per-object capResources are set by the template, not the caller: 'Resources (vcpu, memory, disk) are always loaded from the template. You cannot specify custom resources when creating a sandbox.' A custom template can be built at 'CPU: 1-64 cores, Memory: 512MB-64GB, Disk: 1GB-250GB'.
RetentionA sandbox is destroyed when its timeout expires, and 'All data in the sandbox is lost'; pause and resume preserve state, and a stopped sandbox keeps its filesystem. No retention period for a stopped sandbox is published.
Interfacerest, cli REST API, CLI (hopx) and SDKs (Python, JavaScript/TypeScript), documented; the web console issues the API key.
Toolinghopx CLI (hopx auth, with OAuth login and API key commands); SDKs hopx_ai (Python) and @hopx-ai/sdk (JavaScript/TypeScript); the REST API directly.
Composes withnot established
ReliabilityThe pricing page's own worked example prices a 1 vCPU / 1 GiB / 10 GiB hour at '$0.05', while its rate card gives USD 0.06768 for the same hour, a 26% understatement.
NotesArithmetic, recomputed today: 1 vCPU x USD 0.0504 + 1 GiB x USD 0.0162 + 10 GiB x USD 0.00108 = USD 0.06768 an hour. USD 200 / 0.06768 = 2,955.1 h = 123.1 days of a 1 vCPU / 1 GiB / 10 GiB box at 24/7, and a 730-hour month costs USD 49.41. The vendor's '~4,000 hours' headline counts the vCPU rate only and is 35% optimistic against its own rate card; the record uses the rate card. The vendor's own worked example also understates the same hour at '$0.05' against USD 0.06768. This is the most hours per no-card dollar of the three by a factor of four over E2B and of two and a half over Daytona at its max shape, but the box is 1 vCPU against Daytona's 4, so it is not a faster machine, only a longer-lived one. GPU trap: none to check, because no GPU product is advertised on the pricing page; the rate card is CPU only. The real caveat is identity: the console offers Google and GitHub OAuth ONLY, with no email form, so the account is tied to a Google or GitHub identity rather than an email address. Not published anywhere: the maximum template shape, the maximum timeout, the credit expiry, and any egress allowance. The 30-day runtime claim on the long-running-jobs page is marketing; the docs' own example tops out at 4 hours and no page states a ceiling, so a 730-hour timeout is untested.
Evidence notedocumented Credit, the three unit rates, the OAuth-only console, the template-bound resources and the timeout semantics were each read on a first-party page. The maximum shape and the maximum timeout are not published.
