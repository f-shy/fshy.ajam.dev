The FAQ also names the failure mode that limits the service itself: an error occurs when 'the filehost's premium account has reached daily bandwidth limit'. The leech site is itself a free user of the host.
Bindingrequest-rate Five links a day binds before the 1 GB cap does: at 5 links/day the monthly ceiling is 150 GB even with maximum-size files, and 1 TiB/month would need 34 files a day.
Identityanonymous 'No registration' is stated in the FAQ and on the front page; no account field appears in the generate flow.

free allowance: unstated; the caps are 5 links/day and 1 GB/file

Retentionunverified: the FAQ does not say how long a generated link, or the copy on their server, survives.
Interfaceunknown A single web form: paste the host link, click Generate, receive a premium link. No API documentation was found; a wrapper would drive the form or the resulting page with a browser session.
ToolingNothing published. The FAQ recommends IDM, FDM, JDownloader, wget or curl for the generated link, which is a plain resumable HTTP URL.
Composes withIts output is an ordinary HTTP link, so it feeds any downloader or storage relay. 5 links/day at 1 GB is 5 GB/day of ceiling, which is 150 GB/month at best.
ReliabilityThe leech depends on the vendor's own premium accounts, so it fails when those hit the host's daily bandwidth cap. Ads fund it, and the generated link is a third-party copy, so availability is the vendor's uptime, not the host's.
NotesThis is the clearest published free cap in the sector: 5 links/day, 1 GB/file, no account. It is not a debrid service and it does not touch usenet or torrents; it only converts host links that the vendor's accounts can still fetch.
Evidence notedocumented The caps are quoted verbatim from the site's own FAQ. What is not tested is whether a generation succeeds on 2026-10-23: leechpremium.link did not resolve from this host (DNS failure) and the page was read through network requests.
