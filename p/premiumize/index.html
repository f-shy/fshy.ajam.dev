Point cost is not uniform: the API returns a per-service 'fairusefactor' that multiplies point consumption for that host, so one host can drain the same balance faster than another. The /fairuse page that explains it is behind a login and was not read.
Bindingvolume-quota The only account-level counter is points; a monthly transfer of 1 TiB spends them at a per-host multiplier and hits account_limit_reached, not a connection or per-file limit.
Identityemail 'An e-mail address is required for registration'; disposable-mail domains are refused and the vendor may exclude providers. Card or voucher to pay. No phone number in the pages read.
EgressFair-use points, not bytes: 'The use of the Service is subject to a fair-use limit based on available points ... In cases of excessive usage exceeding available points, access to services is temporarily restricted until the daily replenishment of 30 points at midnight (UTC) or until additional booster points are purchased.'
free allowance: 1,000 points per membership period; 30 points replenished daily at 00:00 UTC

No expiry period is published.

Composes withThe only mainstream debrid with both usenet and 1 TB of retained cloud storage, so it is the archive layer: a completed transfer stays addressable after the transfer list is cleared.
ReliabilityFair-use exhaustion restricts all access until the daily 30-point top-up, not just the offending transfer; a missing key and a wrong key return the same authentication_failed string; a domain-restricted signup can fail before any payment.
NotesPoint exhaustion is documented with a number and a reset time; the per-host multiplier is documented as a field but its values are behind a login; the concurrency ceiling is third-party only. The 30-points-per-day figure is widely quoted as roughly 30 GB/day of ordinary traffic by users, which is an inference from an undisclosed point-to-byte rate and is not stated by the vendor. The cheapest of the eight per month is AllDebrid, but Premiumize is the cheapest that a plain 'rclone mount' can read, and it is the only one with usenet included. The number of supported hosts is not published on any reachable page; premiumize.me/services redirects to /features, which lists capabilities and no host count. The 2026 site dropped the public host-list page entirely; the API endpoint is now the only host list, and it is unauthenticated. The euro price has not moved in 36 months; what changed is the packaging. A 3-month term at EUR 24.99 was sold from 2023 to at least 2025-10 and is gone in 2026, and USD moved to the front of the table at a rate the vendor fixes itself (11.99/9.99 = 1.200, 79.99/69.99 = 1.143), so the dollar buyer pays a different margin than the euro buyer. The EUR buyer's price history is flat. The cleanest documented answer: a lapsed Premiumize keeps nothing by contract. Its 72-hour payment grace is the only slack, and it applies to a failed payment, not to a deliberate pause. Two things go wrong there: a vendor TB is not a TiB (1,000 GB = 0.9095 TiB, so the price per TiB is 10% higher, USD 12.32 not USD 11.21), and the sustainable rate is 30 points a day, which is 900 GB, not 1,000. The 1,000-point ceiling is only reachable once; The best storage-per-euro of the paid multi-hosters and the only one with 1 TB persistent cloud storage plus WebDAV on every plan, but the fair-use balance makes it a 1 TB/month service in practice, and the annual price is the only way it gets near sub-5-euro bar. The coin list and the processor are not published. 'Cryptocurrencies' unnamed is weaker evidence than TorBox's 20-coin list: a client cannot tell in advance whether Monero is accepted, and the privacy property that makes a crypto rail useful depends on the coin.
Evidence notedocumented The 8-request concurrency figure is a third party's and is attributed. A live call spending points would raise the metering half to measured.
