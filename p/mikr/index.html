NotesThe only plan that is genuinely built around IPv6 by design rather than as a way to avoid buying IPv4: the vendor publishes an article explaining that its servers run in 'głównie IPv6' mode, not IPv6-only, and that it deliberately keeps IPv4 scarce so it does not have to buy addresses at about USD 57 each. That is a first-party account of the economics, in Polish, and it is the kind of thing an English search does not surface. The port story is better than the headline suggests and worse than the best rows here: three ports to start, of which two are freely usable, and both of those carry TCP and UDP - the vendor names OpenVPN and WireGuard explicitly - with five more TCP ports free from the panel for a ceiling of seven. At PLN 35 a year gross, which is USD 8.915 a year or USD 0.7429 a month at 3.9261 PLN per USD read, 384 MB and 5 GB NVMe in Finland is good value. Two things hold it to a caveat. The identity story is a bank transfer through a Polish payment processor that passes the buyer's address back to the vendor, and a real first name and surname are required, so this is not an email-only signup; a foreign buyer without a Polish bank account has no published route. And no traffic allowance is published anywhere, which is a documented absence rather than a limit and cannot be used in arithmetic. The VAT ID PL6821639338 is printed in the site metadata and the vendor states it issues VAT invoices, which is more entity disclosure than most rows, though the trading company name behind the VAT number was not read. The separate free FROG product is Poland-only and requires a PLN 5 identity deposit, so it is not available outside Poland. What is not established: the payment step, since the cart was not exercised, and whether a non-Polish bank transfer is accepted.
