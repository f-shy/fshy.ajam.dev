Free tierOracle Always Free includes 1,500 OCPU-hours and 9,000 GB-hours a month plus 10 TB/month of egress, on a card that takes a USD 1.00 authorisation and reverses it. Google Cloud's free tier includes an e2-micro with 1 GB/month of egress. AWS includes 750 public-IPv4 hours a month. Azure and Hetzner record no free allowance: Azure's price cells all render as $- and Hetzner's /29 is sold at EUR 13.60/month plus EUR 34.90 setup.
unit mixed; metered on wall-clock; Allowances combine compute time and bandwidth limits: Oracle and AWS provide hour-denominated allowances (OCPU-hours, GB-hours, 750 hours) alongside byte-denominated egress limits (10 TB monthly, 1 GB monthly). metered_on reflects compute hours as the headline quota, while byte allowances are documented separately.
Pricenot a per-TB product: addresses are billed per hour or per prefix — USD 0.005/h, USD 3.65/month, per address on Google Cloud and AWS; EUR 2.27 per usable IP per month on Hetzner; Azure unverified
Limits
  • retention-clamped (binding) 7-day p95 idle reclaim on Oracle Always Free: the machine is taken back and the address with it source
  • volume-quota Oracle Always Free includes 10 TB/month of egress, about 222 times 45 GB/month source
  • volume-quota Google Cloud free e2-micro includes 1 GB/month of egress, which 45 GB/month exceeds about 45 times over source
  • volume-quota AWS includes 750 public-IPv4 hours a month, which covers one address continuously (744 hours in a 31-day month) with 6 hours spare source
  • volume-quota Google Cloud external IPv4 costs USD 0.005/h while in use, USD 3.65/month, with no free allowance stated source
Three constraints fit none of the seven. Cost: Hetzner's /29 is EUR 13.60 a month plus EUR 34.90 setup, EUR 2.27 per usable IP, and AWS and Google Cloud charge USD 0.005/h per address, USD 3.65 a month. Unverified price: Azure offers a /29 public IP prefix but every price cell on its pricing page renders as hBc, so the price of that prefix is unverified rather than zero. Denominator: the five publish different address products, such as a prefix, a single address, or a subnet, so per-address cost is comparable while capacity is not.
Bindingretention-clamped At 45 GB a month across about 599,000 requests nothing here is near a byte or request ceiling except Google Cloud's 1 GB/month free egress, and crossing that produces a bill rather than a stop; Oracle's 7-day p95 idle reclaim is the only constraint that removes the address, so it is the one that ends the arrangement.
Identitycard Oracle Always Free tier requires a credit card and executes a reversible USD 1.00 authorization. Registration requirements for other listed providers are unverified against recent signup flows.
EgressMetered, with a large free allowance on Oracle and a small one on Google Cloud: Oracle includes 10 TB/month on Always Free, Google Cloud's free e2-micro includes 1 GB/month, and AWS bills the address itself at USD 0.005/h with 750 free hours. Hetzner bills a /29 at EUR 13.60/month plus EUR 34.90 setup. Azure's address prices render as $- and are unverified.
free allowance: Oracle 10 TB/month on Always Free; Google Cloud free e2-micro 1 GB/month; AWS 750 public-IPv4 hours/month
Retentionnot applicable: an address, not an object. The nearest equivalent is the instance holding it: Oracle reclaims an Always Free compute instance that sits at 7-day p95 idle, and the address goes with the machine.
Interfacerest, cli Each provider exposes its own REST API and CLI for address objects, so rest is the nearest token in the closed set and cli is the other half. The schema's enum has no token for a cloud provider API and none for a subnet product. The address is a resource in the account: an external IPv4 on Google Cloud, a public IPv4 on AWS, a public IP prefix on Azure, a subnet on Hetzner, a public IP on an Oracle VNIC.
ToolingThe provider's own REST API, SDK and CLI. No third-party wrapper exists or is needed, because the address is a resource in the account rather than a product with its own protocol. The work is a small driver that creates an instance, attaches the address, and recreates it when the provider takes the machine back. Access is not the problem here.
Composes withOne of these sits behind the Workers AI gateway as the egress hop: the Worker calls the instance, the instance dials out from a cloud address, and because every one of the five is a single ASN the chain buys a second provider rather than a second network.
ReliabilityAll five are datacenter ranges registered to the cloud provider itself or to one of its own entities (ARIN DIRECT ALLOCATION for Oracle, Google Cloud and Azure; ARIN ALLOCATION for AWS; RIPE ASSIGNED PA for Hetzner), so an exit carries that cloud ASN's reputation, and on Oracle the address disappears with the reclaimed machine.
NotesWhat this record is for. The compute topic owns the prices: compute/oracle-cloud, compute/google-cloud-free-tier, compute/aws-free-plan-t4g and compute/hetzner-cloud each carry the free-tier shape and the egress terms, and this record does not correct any of them. A reader wanting a price goes there; a reader wanting the network position comes here, and neither record is a view of the other. One sample, said plainly. The RDAP read is a single address, 129.146.0.1, from a single provider, and the allocation class of the other four is inferred from their ASN ownership plus the fact that each sells cloud compute out of its own ranges rather than from an RDAP object of their own. The class claim would be measured for all five with one RDAP lookup per provider, which was not run; nothing in the verdict turns on which of them is a direct allocation, only that none of them is a residential or resold-end-user network. Denominators, recorded rather than smoothed. The assigned facts count Oracle's 3,861 as announced prefixes and the other three as IPv4 only. Re-read: AS31898 3,861 total and 2,852 v4; AS396982 3,924 total and 3,558 v4; AS16509 25,440 total and 19,416 v4; AS8075 1,355 total and 1,233 v4; AS24940 96 total and 91 v4. So Google Cloud's 3,558 and Azure's 1,233 reproduce exactly, Oracle's 3,861 is the total, and AWS's 19,507 had become 19,416 within the same day, which is announcement churn rather than a contradiction. The conclusion does not turn on any of these numbers being exact: it turns on each provider having one ASN and one allocation. Hetzner's ASN was not in the assigned facts. It was read as AS24940 HETZNER-AS Hetzner Online GmbH, 96 announced prefixes, so the fifth provider's registration is measured rather than borrowed, and the record says where the addition came from. Nothing else was added to the assigned facts. What a static fetch can and cannot re-prove.
Evidence notedocumented The ASN numbers and holders are measured with RIPE prefix-overview and the allocation class is measured for all five providers with one registry lookup each (ARIN for Oracle, Google Cloud, AWS and Azure; RIPE for Hetzner), on one address per provider. The free-tier and price fields are vendor-page reads, so the weakest rung the verdict depends on is documented, not measured.
