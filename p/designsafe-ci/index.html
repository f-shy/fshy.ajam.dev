Free tierNo published cap on published dataset size or file count: 'right now there is no limit to the amount of files that can be published in DesignSafe'. Policies page describes largest published datasets as ~5 TB (CoreTrustSeal submission: 'Publications range up to a maximum of 5TB; seven current published datasets are over 1TB'), while a 2025 Frontiers paper reports individual datasets 'as large as 26' TB. Private working space ('My Data' / 'My Projects') is separate and unlimited in policy terms; uploads of 1 TB+ require a support ticket first.
unit TB; metered on unstated
Pricenone stated
Limits
  • write-gated (binding) approval source
Write access requires a free self-registered TACC account and editorial curation approval. Datasets must fall within natural-hazards engineering and social science research scope. Unauthenticated direct HTTP download paths remain unverified.
Identityemail a TACC account is created at the TACC Accounts form with a first and last name, an email address, a password, and an institution the form looks up by name or accepts typed in, with that institution's country, URL and postal address. The form asks for an email affiliated with the institution; an address whose domain does not match the selected institution is still accepted but 'will require administrative approval', and an institution outside the trusted list gives an account 'limited access to TACC resources' that cannot become a project's principal investigator. No card and no phone number is asked for.
EgressGlobus and HTTPS downloads from public Data Depot. Zip downloads are capped at 2 GB per request. Public metadata API endpoints return complete file manifests with lengths and paths.
Interfacerest rest
ToolingTapis/Agave REST API + dapi Python client (dapi.publications), Globus, JupyterHub, and the Data Depot browser. Public metadata endpoints need no token; the dapi client expects a Tapis token even for public listings, and the unauthenticated /api/files/v2/ media path 404s - so treat the byte-read path as authenticated-or-Globus until proven otherwise.
Composes withPer published project, and there is no documented per-project ceiling, so the multiplier is projects. NSF-funded continuity: data transfers to a successor awardee and TACC commits to keeping landing pages and DOIs indefinitely.
ReliabilityCoreTrustSeal-certified, two-region replication (Corral + Ranch tape). Downloads over ~2 GB need Globus, which is a client install, not a URL.

Free tierA Startup Allocation may request up to 20,000 node-hours annually with fast-track review acted on within two weeks, a Research Allocation up to 100,000 node-hours annually, and an Educational Allocation up to 10,000 node-hours, all provided through NSF funding "at no direct cost to the end user". Unfunded exploratory research is explicitly allowed on a Startup Allocation. Users outside the NSF eligibility categories may buy time from TACC at approximately USD 0.50 per node-hour and storage at approximately USD 60 per TB per year.

Composes withThe purchased route is the composition worth noting: USD 0.50 per node-hour buys a TACC node of 48 to 64 cores, so roughly USD 0.008 to USD 0.01 per core-hour with no affiliation and no review, which is the cheapest way out of the identity gate found among evaluated providers and does not depend on the NSF eligibility categories at all.
Reliabilityno reliability event recorded
NotesThe account is open to anyone, but the account alone does not reach the HPC queue: portal tools are capped by "built-in constraints that limit the amount of compute time" with no published ceiling, and command-line access needs the allocation. Startup review skips the Allocation Advisory Board and is decided within two weeks, which makes 20,000 node-hours the realistic ask rather than the 100,000. Node-hour to core-hour conversion depends on the TACC system assigned, which the page does not name, so no conversion is published here.
