24/7 needs auto_stop_interval=0 and no ttl_minutes; a non-ephemeral VM class defaults to a 60-minute auto-pause when auto-stop is disabled, and auto-pause and auto-stop cannot both be non-zero. The published maximum lifetime of a sandbox is absent.
Bindingretention-clamped The 15-minute idle auto-stop binds first: it fires while processes run, only an external interaction resets it, and it must be set to 0 at create time to keep a machine up.
Identityemail The signup form at auth.daytona.io/sign-up asks for an email address plus 'Continue', or 'Continue with Google', or 'Continue with GitHub'. No phone field and no card field. The pricing page states 'Sign up for a free trial - no credit card required', so no hold exists. Two contrary signals are recorded in notes: the dashboard bundle carries a suspension reason 'Payment method required' that renders the non-dismissible banner 'Add a payment method to start creating sandboxes.', and a preview build of the billing docs says 'Personal organizations receive a one-time grant of free credits when the first card is connected.' The trigger for either is not established.
EgressNetwork limits are applied by tier and are not a metered egress charge. Tier 1 and Tier 2: 'Network access is restricted and cannot be overridden at the sandbox level ... Essential services remain reachable.' Full internet access starts at Tier 3.
Per-object capOne sandbox is capped at 4 vCPU, 8 GiB RAM and 10 GiB disk on every tier: 'Organizations get a maximum sandbox resource limit of 4 vCPUs, 8GB RAM, and 10GB disk'. The default VM snapshot daytona-vm-large is exactly that shape.
RetentionContainer sandboxes keep their filesystem on the runner while stopped and occupy disk quota until archived; VM sandboxes offload filesystem state to storage on stop and free the quota. Nothing is deleted on a timer that the docs publish.
Interfacerest, cli Python, TypeScript, Ruby, Go and Java SDKs over a REST API; SSH access to a sandbox

NotesArithmetic, recomputed today: 4 vCPU x USD 0.0504 + 4 GiB x USD 0.0162 + 5 billed GiB x USD 0.000108 = USD 0.26694 an hour. USD 200 / 0.26694 = 749.2 h = 31.2 days of a 4 vCPU / 4 GiB / 10 GiB VM at 24/7, and a 730-hour month costs USD 194.87, so the credit covers a full month with USD 5.13 to spare. At the maximum shape, 4 vCPU / 8 GiB / 10 GiB, the hour is USD 0.33174 and a 730-hour month is USD 242.17, which the credit does NOT cover: 602.9 h or 25.1 days. The operator's claim is therefore true at 4 GiB of RAM and false at 8 GiB. GPU trap confirmed for this class: the billing page states the free credit balance is 'not available for GPU sandboxes', so the USD 200 buys no GPU minute at any price on the pricing page (H100 USD 2.27/h, RTX 5090 USD 0.74/h). Windows is priced separately at USD 0.0858/vCPU/h and is not excluded by any sentence read. Not published anywhere: the credit's expiry, the sandbox's maximum lifetime, and the contents of the Tier 1 destination allowlist. Also unresolved: the bundle's 'Daytona accounts need a validated work email' screen, which would refuse a personal-domain address. Two tables on the limits page still disagree on Tier 1 memory, 10 GiB against 20 GiB; plan for 10.
Evidence notedocumented Rates, credit, tier table, per-sandbox cap, default VM snapshots, SSH and the Tier 1 network policy were each read on a first-party page and quoted. The pricing page says no; the app bundle and a preview docs build imply a payment-method gate.
