Free tierJetstream2 bills every instance in ACCESS service units: 'a service unit (SU) is equivalent to 1 vCPU for 1 hour of wall clock time'. CPU flavours cost 1 SU per vCPU per hour and Large Memory flavours 2 SU per vCPU per hour; GPU flavours list a flat SU rate per hour. A100 (40 GB GPU RAM): g3.xl, 32 vCPU, 120 GB RAM, 64 SU per hour; g3.large, 16 vCPU, 20 GB GPU RAM, 32 SU per hour; g3.medium, 8 vCPU, 10 GB GPU RAM, 16 SU per hour. L40S (48 GB GPU RAM): g4.xl, 12 vCPU, 84 SU per hour. H100 (80 GB GPU RAM): g5.xl, 20 vCPU, 128 SU per hour. The L40S and H100 flavours are not available by default and need a request with justification. The page lists no A100 80 GB, RTX 4090 or L4 flavour and no dollar price, and states no minimum bill. The smallest ACCESS tier, Explore, carries 400,000 credits, so 400,000 / 64 = 6,250 hours of a full A100 card, 12,500 hours of a half card, 3,125 hours of an H100 card at 128 SU per hour, or 400,000 vCPU-hours on CPU flavours.

Composes withThis is the conversion the ACCESS record lacked: an Explore allocation is not 400,000 abstract credits but 6,250 A100-hours or 400,000 vCPU-hours, which turns the ACCESS tier from a benchmark allowance into roughly a year of a single always-on A100 at 16 hours a day. It composes badly with the free GPU vendors, because the same workload that fits a 40 GB card on Jetstream2 fits a 16 GB T4 elsewhere only after splitting.

NotesThis finding changes compute/access: that record states the 400,000 credit limit but does not convert it, so a reader cannot tell whether Explore buys an afternoon or a year. The division is shown above and uses only rates from the two vendor pages. The cheapest paid shape is not applicable: Jetstream2 sells nothing for cash, and the credits are not purchasable, so the alternative to the institutional gate is not money. Note that stopped instances are still charged 75 percent and shelved instances are charged nothing, which changes the effective hours for an interactive workload.
