Free tier'The base monthly compute quota is determined by your subscription tier. Free tier namespaces receive 400 compute minutes per month.' The pricing page repeats it as '400 compute minutes per month'. A compute minute is not a wall-clock minute: the usage formula is 'Job duration / 60 * Cost factor', and the default small Linux x86-64 runner has cost factor 1, so 400 compute minutes is 400 wall-clock minutes on the default shape and less on any larger one. The card requirement is the finding: 'credit/debit card details are required if you choose to use GitLab.com shared runners', verified with 'a one-dollar authorization transaction'.
unit minutes; metered on wall-clock; 400 compute minutes at cost factor 1 is 6 hours 40 minutes a month, which is 0.22 hours a day. The card gate is on the shared runners specifically, not on the account.

Credit/debit card details are not required if you bring your own runner or disable shared runners.' Verified by 'a one-dollar authorization transaction. No charge will be made and no money will transfer.' source
  • volume-quota (binding) On quota exhaustion, instance runners stop processing new jobs; running jobs continue until the namespace is over quota by 1,000 compute minutes, then they are dropped source
Three separate ceilings and the smallest is the card gate: 400 compute minutes a month, a 3-hour job timeout, and a card required to use the shared runners that the minutes are spent on. Bringing your own runner removes the card requirement and the minute quota at the same time, which needs a machine to run the runner on.
Bindingvolume-quota 400 compute minutes a month is 400 wall-clock minutes on the default small runner at cost factor 1, and instance runners take no new job once it is used; the card check is an identity step, recorded in identity_cost
Identitycard A card, for the thing this record is about. The account itself is free and the pricing page says 'No credit card required' for the Free plan, but the free compute minutes sit behind a card: 'credit/debit card details are required if you choose to use GitLab.com shared runners'. The card is verified by 'a one-dollar authorization transaction' and 'No charge will be made and no money will transfer', so the hold is USD 1.00 authorised and released, not captured. GitLab's identity-verification page adds a second, independent gate that is risk-scored: 'All users - Email verification. Medium-risk users - Phone number verification. High-risk users - Credit card verification.' Whether a new account lands in the medium or high band is not published. Debit acceptance is not stated on any page read; the pages say 'credit/debit card' without distinguishing.

NotesThe record is card, with a USD 1.00 authorisation hold and release. The pricing page's 'No credit card required' line is true of the account and false of the thing the record is about. GitLab's own FAQ says the card is required 'if you choose to use GitLab.com shared runners'. Shared runners are the only way to spend the 400 minutes without a self-managed runner. Identity verification on the GitLab free tier is a risk-scored three-stage scheme, and it is documented. Separately and unconditionally, the shared runners need a card. Not established: whether a new account from a given country lands in the medium-risk (phone) or high-risk (card) band, because the risk score is not published; and whether a debit or prepaid card passes the one-dollar authorisation, because no page says. The 400 minutes figure is confirmed.
