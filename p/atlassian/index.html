Free tierFree (up to 5 users): 1 GB LFS storage. Academic 5 GB, Standard 5 GB, Premium 10 GB. Extra storage sold in 100 GB units at $10/month. Notable counter-property: no per-file cap at all.
unit GB; metered on stored-bytes
Pricenone stated
Limits
  • per-object-cap (binding) no per-file cap at all source
'there's no limit on the LFS file size you can push to Bitbucket Cloud'. Genuinely no per-file cap. Forks do not copy LFS objects (fork points at parent's store, and the fork lacks permission). So fork-based sharding fails. Repo archives do not include LFS files.
Identitycard (inferred, not read) not published
EgressNot metered. Atlassian publishes no LFS bandwidth quota, only storage in 100 GB units. This is the one LFS host where the storage number is small but the transfer side is genuinely unstated/unbilled.
Per-object capno per-file cap at all
Interfacessh, git git
ToolingGit-lfs. Note: SSH not usable for LFS repos? (not stated). The documented limitation is that repository transfer and forking both break LFS links; the alternative route is git lfs fetch --all + git lfs push --all.
Composes with1 GB per free workspace, unlimited free workspaces (each needs an email). 500 workspaces = 500 GB of very awkward storage. Weak: no route to manage many workspaces at once, and the workspace is tied to users.
ReliabilityFork/transfer/archive are all documented as broken for LFS repos, which makes recovery awkward. Storage overage is auto-billed in 100 GB increments rather than blocked.
