Free tier'We give $1k cash grants to students and people early in their careers to build projects and prototypes advancing the edge of scientific research', described in the fund's own post as 'a minimum $1K stipend'. Terms are genuinely unconditional: 'This is a no strings attached grant - we don't own you, your project, any IP generated, future equity in anything you generate, etc. It's a Venmo payment.' The route is an email with 'a five minute Loom video' to any partner at the fund. Eligibility is by career stage rather than by institution: 'high school, college students, and dropouts'. The geographic limit is stated plainly.

Identitycard the fund states it cannot pay outside North America: '1517 supports hackers and makers all over the world through community and mentorship but unfortunately due to international regulations and rules, we are unable to issue grants to teams based outside of North America.' The payment rail named is Venmo, which is US-only, and the post also mentions cheques.

NotesA REFUTATION of the shape rather than of the programme: this is the cleanest illustration in the class that 'no strings attached' and 'anywhere in the world' are separate claims, and that the payment rail is the one that decides reachability. The fund is candid about it, which is why the record can be documented rather than inferred. Note the parent fund's own page does not state an upper bound and describes the award as a minimum, so 'at least USD 1,000' is the correct reading; no published example of a larger Medici award was found.
