Free tierNo storage quota on the Standard tier. It is priced at $0/GB/month by design: 'Standard — Stored data: Free — Durable, high-capacity storage. The default'. The paid tiers are Performance at $0.015/GB/month and Accelerated at $0.02/GB/month. There is no published cap on bytes stored, only 'availability varies — a tier can show as out of stock'.
unit unlimited; metered on stored-bytes
PriceUSD 0/TB/month stored on Standard; egress USD 0.01/GB (USD 10/TB); Performance USD 0.015/GB/month (USD 15/TB/month); Accelerated USD 0.02/GB/month (USD 20/TB/month)
Limits
  • write-gated (binding) A funded credit balance is a stated prerequisite of object storage, and the vendor's object-storage documentation put the minimum at least USD 0.01 credit; credit is bought up front with a payment card or USDC, so an unfunded account owns no workspace and therefore no bucket source
Credit on the account is a stated prerequisite, bought with a payment card or USDC; the vendor's object-storage documentation put the minimum at least USD 0.01 and the current page states the prerequisite without a number, so the live console's minimum is unverified behind a sign-up. The console create form prices the three tiers and creates the workspace, and only then does a bucket exist. Tier availability varies: a tier can show as out of stock. Bucket names get a platform prefix (u-<id>-<workspace>.<bucket>), so the name the S3 tools see is read from the console. No per-object cap is published.
Bindingwrite-gated the gate is money, not identity: sign-up costs an email address and grants Tier 1, and nothing is stored until a credit purchase funds the account, payable by card or in USDC. Egress at USD 0.01/GB is the recurring cost, not a limit on creation.
Identityemail email and password, or a Google account, then an activation email; object storage needs credit bought in advance with a payment card or USDC, so no card is needed when paying in USDC, and the vendor's object-storage documentation put the minimum at least USD 0.01 of credit for the workspace that holds the buckets; one payment card cannot be added to more than one account
EgressOutgoing traffic is billed at $0.01 per GB on all tiers while incoming traffic is included. At $0.01 per GB ($10 per TB), reading 20 TB costs approximately $200, and a continuous 500 GB to 1 TiB daily transfer volume costs $150 to $300 monthly.
price per tb: $10/TB
Interfaces3, cli part of the text of Limits, above
endpoint: s3-ca-east.nebulablock.com:443 over HTTPS, path-style addressing, Signature V4; each workspace shows its own Hostname in the S3 Credentials panel of its Overview tab
ToolingReal S3: s3cmd, boto3, AWS SDKs, documented setup pages per OS and per language. A workspace issues an access key pair; buckets live inside it and are private by default, switchable to public, in which case 'objects can be accessed directly via permanent URL'. Platform API exposes usage reporting (24h/7d/30d) and key rotation, and usage is metered hourly.
Composes withWorkspaces are the multiplier and nothing published caps how many you create. The genuinely interesting property is the price inversion: at $0 storage and $10/TB egress this is a very cheap place to PARK 20 TB (write-once, rare read = the cold-archive workload) and an expensive place to serve from. So the correct chain is Nebula Block as the backing store, with Cloudflare in front so the paid egress is consumed once by the cache rather than once per request.
