'Works with 155+ hosts'; no monthly cap, no concurrency figure, no per-host limits published.
Bindingvolume-quota Money is the counter: every byte is bought, so at 1 TiB a month the constraint is the invoice rather than a rate or a shaping rule. The site publishes no per-GB rate, so the invoice cannot be predicted without submitting a file.
Identityanonymous 'no signup' and 'no accounts to create'; the download link is emailed, so an email address is optional rather than required, and payment is by crypto or PayPal.
EgressEach download is priced individually; balance top-ups carry a bonus (+10% from $10, +15% from $20, +20% from $50) and paying from balance is fee-free.
price per tb: not published as a rate; priced per file
Per-object capnot stated: the price is computed per file from its size ('we check the file and price in real time')
Retentionnot stated; the link is shown on-page and emailed
Interfaceunknown Web form only; payment by Crypto (Cryptomus: USDT, BTC, ETH) or PayPal. No API is documented.
ToolingBrowser only; no download-manager integration named.
Composes withIt is the only service here that turns a single file into a known cash price before download, which makes it the fallback when a subscription's host list fails and one file must still arrive.
ReliabilityPay-per-file removes subscription risk but leaves balance risk: a top-up bonus encourages prepaying into a wallet held by an operator with no status page.
NotesThe absence of a published per-GB rate is the finding: the same service sells files at a price it computes at submission time, so a client cannot budget a terabyte month without sampling. Useful as a one-file fallback, not as a base.
