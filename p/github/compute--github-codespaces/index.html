Free tier'All GitHub personal accounts include a quota of free compute time and storage for GitHub Codespaces.' The table row is '| GitHub Free for personal accounts | 15 GB-month | 120 hrs |'. The 120 is a core-hour: the compute column is multiplied by the machine's core count, so 120 core-hours is 60 wall-clock hours on the 2-core default. Quoted: 'The compute cost is proportional to the number of processor cores in the machine type you choose for your codespace, as shown in the following table. For example, the compute cost of using a codespace for an hour on a 16-core machine is eight times greater than a 2-core machine.'
unit hours; metered on wall-clock; The vendor's own header for the column is 'Compute time per month' and the value is 120 hrs, but the included-usage multiplier makes it core-hours. 120 core-hours / 2 cores = 60 hours of the default machine.
Pricenone stated
Limits
  • volume-quota (binding) 120 core-hours of compute a month on GitHub Free, which is 60 wall-clock hours on the 2-core default machine and 30 hours on a 4-core source
  • volume-quota (binding) 15 GB-month of storage a month on GitHub Free; beyond it, USD 0.07 per GB-month source
  • retention-clamped 'A codespace will stop running after a period of inactivity. By default this period is 30 minutes' and 'Regardless of your idle timeout setting, a codespace has a maximum lifetime of 12 hours.' source
  • retention-clamped 'By default, GitHub Codespaces are automatically deleted after they have been stopped and have remained inactive for 30 days.' Automatic deletion happens irrespective of unpushed changes. source
  • write-gated 'If your account does not have a valid payment method on file, usage is blocked once you use up your quota.' A card is the price of continuity past the quota, not of entry. source
  • write-gated The terms scope the machine away from serving. Quoted: 'Any activity that places a burden on our servers, where that burden is disproportionate to the benefits provided to users (for example, don't use Codespaces as a content delivery network, as part of a serverless application, or to host any kind of production-facing application)'. source
120 core-hours is the number that binds. At the 2-core default it is 60 hours a month, which is 2 hours a day; at 4 cores it is 30 hours a month, which is 1 hour a day. The 12-hour maximum lifetime per session and the 30-minute default idle stop are what keep the quota from being burned in one sitting.
Bindingvolume-quota 120 core-hours is 60 hours on the smallest machine, so at 24/7 the month is gone in two and a half days. The quota is what fails, not identity and not the clock.
Identityemail a GitHub account and nothing else: no card, no phone and no identity document to create a codespace. No hold exists because no card is taken. The gate is at the quota, not at signup: 'If your account does not have a valid payment method on file, usage is blocked once you use up your quota.' Debit acceptance is not stated on any page read.
Egressno egress allowance or per-byte price published on any page read; the billing page prices compute per core-hour and storage per GB-month and says nothing about transfer
Per-object capnot applicable: the unit is a session, not an object
Retention'Inactive codespaces are automatically deleted. You can choose how long your stopped codespaces are retained, up to a maximum of 30 days.' and 'By default, GitHub Codespaces are automatically deleted after they have been stopped and have remained inactive for 30 days.' and 'Automatic deletion happens irrespective of whether a codespace contains unpushed changes.' The disk persists across a stop and start until that clock runs out. (30 days)
Interfacerest, cli gh codespace ssh and the VS Code or browser client; a devcontainer defines the image
Toolinggh CLI, the Codespaces API, devcontainer.json, prebuilds
Composes withthe cheapest path to a real Linux dev machine with a shell that a GitHub identity already opens
Reliabilitya codespace stops on an account-level idle timeout (30 minutes by default, settable between 5 and 240 minutes) and is deleted 30 days after it is stopped, irrespective of unpushed changes
NotesThe core-hour unit is the trap: 120 reads like hours and is not, because it is spent per core, so the wall-clock allowance halves at 4 cores and quarters at 8. The shape is quoted from the overview page as the bottom of the machine-type range: 2 cores, 8 GB RAM, 32 GB storage, and the vendor writes decimal GB, kept unconverted. Identity is email, not card: the gate is at the quota, so a reader who plans one month never meets a card and a reader who plans a second month does. Two things this record does not establish. First, the egress term: no page read publishes a quota, a shaping tier or a price for transfer, so the field is a dash and not 'unlimited'.
Evidence notedocumented the quota, the storage allowance, the per-core multiplier and the payment-method block are quoted from GitHub's billing page read; the idle timeout, the 12-hour lifetime and the 30-day retention are quoted from GitHub's own how-to pages read; the 2-core shape is quoted from the Codespaces overview. The egress term is not published anywhere read and is recorded as absent.
