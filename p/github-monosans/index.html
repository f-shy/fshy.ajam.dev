Bindingretention-clamped Nothing the vendor meters fails first: no key, no account, no published rate limit and no byte quota exist to hit. The ceiling is how long an address stays usable. 29% of a verified working set was still working 22 minutes later, and 11 of the 21 addresses working at the second check had failed at the first, so the list has to be re-pulled and re-verified far more often than the vendor's hourly refresh, and that churn rather than any allowance is what limits the gateway.

ToolingA single curl, or any HTTP client, pulls the JSON; no account, key, SDK or wrapper is involved. Filtering by registration class is a few lines against the asn.autonomous_system_number field, which is the point of this list: the choice can be made before a request is spent. Using an address afterwards needs a client that speaks its protocol, which curl -x and rclone --http-proxy both do for http and socks5, and which curl -x socks4 does for the SOCKS4 rows.
Composes withA supply of egress addresses for the Workers AI gateway that can be filtered before use: the row's ASN lets the measured exits be split into consumer ISP and mobile networks against hosting, which is the split no other free list in this topic publishes, so the gateway can aim at a registration class instead of taking whatever the list returns.
ReliabilityOpen proxies of unknown ownership that churn as well as shrink: 35 of 100 sampled addresses carried a payload at the first check and 21 of 100 22 minutes later, with 25 stopping and 11 starting, and 24 of the 59 measured exits sat in consumer ISP or mobile ASNs (China Mobile AS9808, Viettel AS7552, Cox AS22773, Biznet AS17451, TATTELECOM AS28840, Superonline AS34984) rather than in datacentres.
NotesThe verdict rests on (2) with (3) open. Intersected on host and port, 10 of the 35 addresses that worked at the first check worked at the second, which is 29%, and 11 of the 21 working at the second check had failed at the first. Both results files carry host and port, so the intersection is reproducible in one pass, and the difference matters because a reader planning a pull schedule acts on the survival number and not on the yield ratio. The 59 exit addresses are not the mono sample alone: the mono runs produced 39 distinct exits between them and 29 of those appear in this set, so the file aggregates measured exits from the whole lane and the composition figures describe that population. Why this list is qualifies-with-caveat while ip-proxy/proxyscrape-free-list is dead at a comparable yield, 32% against 35%: the ASN field. Proxyscrape publishes no ASN, so its addresses are indistinguishable until one is spent; here the registration class is on the row, which is rung 5 reachable before the first request, and that is the whole difference between a blind pool and a pool that can be aimed. Weight: each rate is one pair of runs over 100 of 1,751 lines, so the churn is a point estimate with no interval, though 25 addresses stopping against 11 starting is a net fall the sample is large enough to show. None exists outside .tmp, so this record corrects nothing outside itself.
Evidence notemeasured Every field the verdict rests on was measured on 2026-10-24: the anonymous unauthenticated fetch, the 1,751 rows and their protocol split, the per-row ASN and geolocation fields, and the two payload rates over the identical 100 endpoints. The hourly regeneration is documented from the publisher's own schedule and was not observed; the verdict does not turn on it, because the 22-minute decay already bounds the usable window. Moving up would need a larger sample and runs spread across a day, so that a diurnal effect can be told apart from a list that is simply thin.
