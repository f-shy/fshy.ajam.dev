Uploading into the user's own accounts means the destination host's quota, not Mirrorcreator's, is what runs out first. Anonymous destinations avoid that at the cost of the files being outside the account's control.
Bindingnone Mirrorcreator stores nothing itself, so no byte counter of its own can bind at 1 TiB. The constraint moves to whichever destination host is selected.
Identityemail Uploads can be anonymous where a destination allows it, or made into the user's own accounts ('if the hosting sites support anonymous file uploads, you can even upload files without the need for registration'). No Mirrorcreator account was required on the page read.
EgressDownloads come from the destination hosts, so their limits apply, not Mirrorcreator's. The service itself publishes no byte allowance and no price.

Composes withThe inverse of a debrid: instead of pulling one link from many hosts, it pushes one file to many hosts.
