Address distribution: 566 relay servers resolve to 258 /24 subnets across 27 autonomous systems. Two networks represent 61 /24 subnets each: AS9009 (M247) at 23.6% and AS11878 (tzulo, inc.) at 23.6%. Bindingrequest-rate The 5-device cap fails first: at about 45 GB a month across about 599,000 requests no byte quota exists to hit and no rate was observed to be shaped, so what bounds it is how many devices one account may register - five exits held at once, and a sixth means a second EUR 5 account. The nearest alternative in the vocabulary is per-ip-shaping, which the sibling ip-proxy/nordvpn-exits uses for its 8-connection ceiling, and the two labels describe the same shape, a ceiling on how many sessions one account may hold rather than a ceiling on bytes or on requests per second.

ToolingAn HTTP client is the whole of it for the relay list, and the list names each relay's fqdn and its SOCKS5 name, so a client pins a hostname rather than a country. Reaching an exit needs an account registered to a device and a dialler that runs on the local machine; a Cloudflare Worker cannot open a WireGuard tunnel, so that dialler has to be written. No wrapper for relay selection exists and that is a tooling note, not a reason to reject the provider.
Composes withA Worker or a free compute node dials one named relay and the request leaves at that relay's address, so the target sees a hosting ASN instead of Cloudflare's, and because 27 ASNs are reachable through one account the rotation is across relays rather than across subscriptions. The 5-device cap bounds how many of those relays can be held open at once, so the rotation is sequential on five slots, not parallel over 566 hosts.
ReliabilityMullvad ended port forwarding on 2023-07-01, precluding inbound routing. Infrastructure composition: 124 of 566 relays are vendor-owned, while 442 operate in third-party hosting facilities. Shared exit IP blocks belong to datacenter and hosting network classes.
NotesWhat makes it work here and not elsewhere is the pair of numbers beside it: the list needs no account, and 2.19 relays per /24 means a hostname is nearly an address, so naming a host names a network. NordVPN's list is also public and also gives hostnames, but at 9.3 relays per /24 a hostname there is a pick inside a network and not a pick of one; Proton's equivalent listing refuses unauthenticated reads, which is what ip-proxy/protonvpn-exits records from its own probe of /vpn/logicals. (3) Relay count close to address count: 566 relays over 258 /24s is 2.19 relays per /24, against NordVPN's 9.3, so reading the headline count overstates the reachable networks by a factor of two rather than by 288.
