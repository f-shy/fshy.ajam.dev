Thread-bound, not byte-bound: 'the thread limit is equal to the number of threads you purchase'. 100 threads must aggregate 12.7 MB/s for 1 TiB/day, which requires ~1 Mbit/s sustained per thread - plausible on paper, unverified in practice and dependent on which consumer-connection IPs are live. Latency and availability are whatever the volunteer hosts happen to be. Cycling consumer-connection IPs will meet automated-traffic filters on many origins.
Bindingper-ip-shaping one stream is shaped, and aggregate throughput is the shaped rate times the number of streams

Interfacerclone HTTP/HTTPS/SOCKS5/UDP forward proxy; rotating or sticky ports (10/20/30 min); country/city/ISP targeting
Toolingany HTTP client with proxy support - curl -x, requests, rclone --http-proxy, aria2
Composes withthis is the only flat-rate egress product found in the entire research: $150/month buys an unmetered HTTP egress path, so 1 TiB/day costs $5/TB of egress regardless of volume. It is a forward proxy, not a CDN - nothing is cached and every byte is re-fetched from origin through a stranger's home connection.
ReliabilityExit nodes are consumer connections that go offline without notice; origins frequently block or add a human-verification step to consumer-connection proxy ranges.
