The 1-hour continuous runtime is the headline difference between Hobby and the USD 150 a month Pro plan, so the time cap is a plan gate rather than a technical ceiling. It resets on pause and resume.
Bindingretention-clamped One hour of continuous runtime per session is the constraint that decides the row: no arrangement of the credit produces a machine that is up for a month, only 604 one-hour sessions.
Identityemail The console signup at console.e2b.dev/registration renders 'Sign up with a social provider or your email.' with 'Continue with GitHub', 'Continue with Google', or fields for 'E-Mail', 'Name' and 'Password'. No phone field and no card field. The pricing page states 'No credit card required to start.' No hold exists because no payment method is taken at signup.
EgressNo egress charge or allowance is stated on the pricing page; sandbox storage is stated free and the page prices CPU and RAM only. Outbound traffic leaves from ephemeral rotating IPs with no published CIDR list.
Per-object capSandboxes can be configured with 1 to 8 vCPUs and 1 to 8 GiB of RAM; the default sandbox has 2 vCPUs and 4 GiB. Storage is 10 GiB on Hobby.
RetentionSandbox storage is included with every plan at no extra cost; a paused sandbox keeps its filesystem and its memory. 'Paused sandbox retention: Unlimited' on every plan.
Interfacerest, cli SDK (Python, JavaScript) over a REST API; Sandbox.create() plus pause and resume
Toolinge2b Python and Node SDKs; templates; sandbox pause and resume
Composes withpause keeps memory, so a long agent run resumes mid-process rather than replaying its setup
ReliabilityA sandbox that hits the one-hour limit is destroyed and its in-memory state is lost unless it was paused or snapshotted first; the default timeout when none is set is 5 minutes.
NotesArithmetic, recomputed today: 2 vCPU x USD 0.0504 + 4 GiB x USD 0.0162 + 0 storage = USD 0.1656 an hour. USD 100 / 0.1656 = 603.9 h = 25.2 days at 24/7, and a 730-hour month would cost USD 120.89. But those 604 hours cannot be taken as a continuous machine: Hobby caps continuous runtime at 1 hour, so they are 604 sessions of one hour or less, and the credit is the smaller constraint only in the sense that it runs out second. At the maximum shape, 8 vCPU / 8 GiB, the hour is USD 0.5328 and USD 100 buys 187.7 h = 7.8 days, still in one-hour slices. GPU trap: not applicable, because E2B states it sells no GPU at all, so there is no advertised product the credit fails to cover. The real catch is the card AFTER the credit, not before: 'If you have no payment method, every project in your workspace is blocked shortly afterwards. Add a payment method to continue using E2B.' That is a hard stop at the end of the USD 100 with no free path onward. Pausing is unlimited and free, so a paused sandbox can sit indefinitely, but it is not running. One conflict to note: the billing docs' cost advice says the default is '2 vCPU, 512 MiB RAM' while the pricing page says 4 GiB; if 512 MiB is right the hour is USD 0.1089 and the credit buys 918 h, so the shape should be checked in the console before trusting either figure.
