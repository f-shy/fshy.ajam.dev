Bindingper-ip-shaping 10 NAT ports is the tightest number on the offer and the one a WireGuard-plus-web-server combination will run out of first; transfer is unlimited and the price is not a constraint.
Identityemail no card at registration. The account page at dash.reconv.pl/en/auth asks for an email address and a password only - the form's two inputs are placeholder 'Enter your email address' and 'Please enter your password' - and no card, address, phone or ID field appears. Payment is a separate vPLN balance top-up after the account exists; the payment rails the vendor accepts are Credit card, PayPal, SEPA, UnionPay and Wire transfer per the Server Hunter listing of the vendor's own feed, which was not confirmed on a first-party checkout page because the checkout is behind the account. No card hold or authorisation amount is stated anywhere read.

Composes withPLN 3.76 a month at the annual rate, or USD 0.958, for 512 MB and 6 GB with unlimited transfer, which is the only provider among documented hosts that publishes unlimited traffic at this price; the 10 NAT ports and the vendor's own TeamSpeak-over-UDP wiki page make it usable as a voice or tunnel endpoint.
Reliabilityno reliability event observed. The vendor states DDoS protection on every plan and no SLA is published.
NotesTwo prices exist and they disagree: the vendor's own order page charges PLN 4.00 a month, while Server Hunter lists PLN 3.80 for the semi-annual interval and PLN 3.68 for the annual one. The vendor's page is the authority and the Server Hunter figures are recorded as the mirror of the vendor's feed, not as the price. The monthly figure was taken as the headline because it needs no prepay; the annual is PLN 45.12, which is PLN 3.76 a month. The vendor's own order page states its own conversion, '1 USD ~= 3.92 PLN', which is within 0.2 percent of the rate used here, so the conversion is not doing any work the vendor would dispute. Not established: the exact count of UDP ports among the 10 (the wiki implies at least one UDP port is forwarded but does not state the split), and the checkout payment rails first-hand, because the confirmation step needs an account. The order flow is a two-step wizard whose second step is 'Login / Registration', so a human reaches registration from the order page itself.
