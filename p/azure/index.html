Free tierThe answer to the ten-minute-checkpoint question, computed from published inputs rather than measured. Take a checkpoint interval of 10 minutes, so the expected work lost per preemption is 5 minutes. With a preemption rate of 2.3 per cent an hour, the overhead is 0.19 per cent and the effective A100 80 GiB rate is USD 0.680 an hour. At 4.1 per cent an hour the overhead is 0.34 per cent and the rate is USD 0.681. Even at AWS's reported 17.5 per cent an hour for p4d, the overhead is 1.46 per cent, which raises USD 5.7337 to USD 5.8173. Survival of a one-hour run is 97.7 per cent at 2.3 per cent an hour, 96.0 per cent at 4.1 per cent, and 83.9 per cent at 17.5 per cent; over ten hours the same three are 79.5, 66.4 and 17.4 per cent.
unit hours; metered on wall-clock; none

NotesNo provider publishes a run-survival figure. Azure publishes a per-hour eviction probability per SKU and region through Resource Graph over 28 trailing days, and GCP publishes a preemption rate through advice.capacityHistory, but neither is a survival curve and the two are not the same statistic: GCP divides preempted VMs by VMs that stopped running, which counts user-caused stops in the denominator and so understates the rate a long job experiences. GCP also publishes an estimated uptime in three buckets only (60 minutes, 10 minutes, 1 minute) from advice.capacity, which is the closest thing to a published survival figure and is coarse.
