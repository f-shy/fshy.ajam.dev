The about page's own changelog names the constraint the marketing hides: '403: User Rate Limit Exceeded - Fixed to Great Extent'. The phrase 'to great extent' is the operator admitting the fix is partial.
Bindingper-ip-shaping A client pulling 1 TiB a month through DriveSeed pulls it from Google, and Google's per-file and per-session shaping is what stops the transfer. The site adds no counter that could bind first.
Identityanonymous Downloading needs nothing. Publishing or managing a Drive needs Google OAuth with drive scope: 'You have to Sign Up through google and allow our application the Permissions to access and manage your google drive.'
EgressDownloads are served by the site as one-click links, 'No annoying waiting times'. No allowance, price or meter is printed.
Per-object capNot stated on the site. The only size-shaped statement is that the service plays and shares video stored in the owner's Drive.
RetentionNot stated for the links. The about page claims 'New Layers of Backup Drive System Added to Keep Files Alive', which is about the operator's re-upload behaviour, not a retention promise to a user.
Interfaceunknown Web only as read: a dashboard, 'Sign in using Google+' OAuth, drive browsing, bulk link export, and a documented 'Download Folder Change Option' in the profile panel. No API, RSS or CLI was found on the pages read.
ToolingThird-party bypass scripts exist for the whole family (bypasser.py in Link-Bypasser-Bot lists driveseed among the gdlist hosts), which is the de-facto API. No first-party client.
Composes withThe last wrapper before the bytes: an index site posts a DriveSeed link, the downloader lands on a Google-hosted file, and the link survives re-uploads because the operator keeps backup Drives. It sits behind FilePress and HubCloud in the same chain and is interchangeable with GDFlix, which ships the same page.
ReliabilityThe service is a Google OAuth app holding drive scope over the user's account, so a publisher's exposure is the whole Drive, not one folder. Links depend on the operator's backup Drives staying alive, and the site has rotated versions (v5.1 today, with a 'Fully New Dark Theme' rewrite) without a stable public changelog.
NotesThe unlimited claim is the one number on the page and it is the one published records must not repeat as a capability: Drive is the host and Drive meters.
Evidence notedocumented Homepage and about page read on the live host. The 'unlimited' claim is the vendor's, unmeasured: no download was pulled and no quota was seen to fail. What would raise the rung is one scripted fetch of a large shared file with the byte count and the throttle status.
