NotesThe best port story in the band and the weakest entity story. 999 TCP and UDP ports on one shared IPv4 removes the entire problem the other NAT sellers have: no port budget, no mapping panel, no ticket, so a WireGuard listener can sit on its default port. 1 GB and 10 GB of NVMe for 59 RUB is USD 0.7257 a month at the rate read, which is 1.8 times the C-Servers monthly rate for 2.7 times the RAM. Two things hold it back. The shared IPv4 is explicitly not permanent: the offer reserves the right to reassign it at any time and tells the buyer to update their own DNS when it happens, which for a WireGuard endpoint means reissuing every peer's config. And the seller has no findable legal entity: the offer is signed '© Cheap-Host', prints no company name, no INN or OGRN and no address, and the domain was registered on 2026-06-14, four months before this reading. That is not the dissolved-company pattern - there is no company to dissolve - but it is also not an entity that can be checked, and evaluated offerings brief asks for exactly that check. Payment is the other wall: SBP, SberPay, YooMoney and MIR cards are Russian domestic rails, so a buyer outside Russia is left with cryptocurrency. Not established: the entity, whether a non-Russian buyer can pay at all, the stock of the NAT Start rung, and whether the 999 ports are reachable end to end rather than advertised.
