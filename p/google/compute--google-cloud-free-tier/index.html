NotesSIGNUP PATH, read. There is no Google Cloud signup page: cloud.google.com/signup returns a 404 and console.cloud.google.com/freetrial redirects to a Google account signup at accounts.cloud.google.com, whose first field is an email address. The Cloud form comes after the Google account exists and asks for name, address and payment method, and in some countries a bank account instead. A card is not optional: 'you must provide a credit card or other payment method'. THE HOLD IS 0.00 TO 1.00 USD, published on the free-features page, and it is the widest published range of the three providers: a bank may convert it and hold it for 'a few days to a month'. THE DEBIT RULE has a second half that matters more than the first: debit is accepted with a Visa or Mastercard logo, but 'Debit cards that require two-factor authentication aren't accepted for payment', which excludes most European and many Asian debit cards that push a one-time password by SMS. Prepaid and virtual are refused. THE GRANT IS TWO PRODUCTS. The always-free e2-micro is a permanent monthly allowance. The USD 300 is a 90-day trial on the same billing account, and when it closes Google stops 'all of its associated projects and resources' - the documentation does not carve out the always-free instance the way Oracle's FAQ does for Always Free. A user who wants the e2-micro to survive month four has to upgrade to a paid billing account, and then the always-free allowance still covers the instance and the bill is zero, but the account is now billable and the 1 GB egress overage is chargeable. That is the caveat, and it is the difference between this row and Oracle's. WHAT IT IS USABLE FOR: 1 GB a month of egress out of North America is 0.09 per cent of one TiB (1e9 bytes against 1.0995e12), so it is a relay, a scheduler, a monitoring node or an SSH jump box, and not a host. Inbound listen works and it has a public ephemeral IP, not a static one; a static IP is billed separately. The shape is 0.25 sustained vCPU with a 30-second burst, so anything CPU-bound is slow. Not established: whether a phone number is required at signup, because no page read names one and the console form is behind a Google login; the phrase 'Google accounts with only a phone number are not supported' is about the account type, not about a verification step.
Evidence notedocumented The grant, the egress term, the shape, the card rules and the hold each come from a first-party page read and quoted on 2026-10-24. The signup field list is documented at the FAQ level; the console form itself is behind a Google login, so the phone question is left unverified rather than guessed.
